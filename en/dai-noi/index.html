    Hue Imperial City

    The Nguyen dynasty’s imperial citadel in Hue, the heart of the UNESCO-listed monuments.

    At a glance

    Location

    Central Hue, about 2 hours from Da Nang by car

    Time needed

    2–3 hours

    Ticket (guide)

    About 200,000 VND for adults (may change)

    Highlights

    • The Meridian Gate and the Thai Hoa Palace for imperial ceremonies
    • A large site with the Forbidden City remains and ancestral temples
    • Photos in royal costumes are popular

    About the Imperial City

    Hue was the capital of the Nguyen dynasty from 1802 to 1945, and its palace is the Imperial City (Dai Noi). Construction began around 1804 under the first emperor, Gia Long. The design draws on China’s Forbidden City but also reflects Vietnamese feng shui ideas. In 1993 the Complex of Hue Monuments was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

    The vast walls are surrounded by a moat and pierced by many gates. Inside, palaces for imperial ceremonies, temples to past emperors, gardens and living quarters were laid out in an orderly way. You can walk through and imagine the life and power of the old dynasty.

    Meridian Gate and Thai Hoa Palace

    The Meridian Gate (Ngo Mon) at the front is a grand gateway that serves as the palace’s face. The central door is said to have been reserved for the emperor, and important ceremonies were held in the pavilion above. Climb up for a view of the wide forecourt and the citadel.

    Beyond the gate, the Thai Hoa Palace was the central hall for the emperor’s coronation and major ceremonies. Inside the red-and-gold timber building stand a gilded throne and columns decorated with dragons. It lets you feel the splendour and dignity of the court up close.

    The Forbidden City and other sights

    Deep inside the palace stood the Forbidden Purple City, where only the emperor and his family lived. Many buildings were lost to war, but the sites and restored halls let you imagine how it looked. The heavy damage in the 1968 Tet Offensive fighting is also an important part of the place’s story.

    The grounds also include the temple honouring past emperors, the Nine Dynastic Urns, and the royal theatre. On some days court music and costume experiences are offered, bringing dynastic culture closer.

    Getting there and time needed

    It stands on the north bank of the Perfume River in Hue and is easy to reach by car or taxi from city hotels. From Da Nang it takes about 2 to 2.5 hours by car, so leave early for a day trip. Allow 2 to 3 hours, or half a day if you want to take your time.

    Summer sun is intense with little shade, so go in the morning or late afternoon. Stone paving is slippery when wet, so wear comfortable shoes and watch your step.

    Suggested plan and food

    A classic day is to tour the Imperial City in the morning, enjoy Hue cuisine rooted in the royal kitchen at lunch, and visit tombs along the Perfume River in the afternoon. Try Hue specialities such as bun bo Hue, spicy beef noodle soup, banh beo and banh khoai.

    Among Hue’s tombs, those of Khai Dinh, Tu Duc and Minh Mang are especially popular. To visit several in a day, a tour with a car and a Japanese- or Korean-speaking guide is convenient.

    Planning tips

    The grounds are large, so bring comfortable shoes, a hat and water. Cover shoulders and knees in palaces and temples. Photography is allowed in many places, but do not enter areas marked as restricted.
