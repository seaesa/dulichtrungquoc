    Khai Dinh Tomb

    The tomb of the 12th emperor, known for its blend of Eastern and Western decoration.

    At a glance

    Location

    About 10 km from Hue city

    Time needed

    About 1 hour

    Ticket (guide)

    About 150,000 VND for adults (may change)

    Highlights

    • An ornate interior decorated with ceramic and glass pieces
    • A compact tomb built up a hillside with steep steps
    • One of the most distinctive tombs in Hue

    About Khai Dinh’s Tomb

    Khai Dinh’s Tomb is the mausoleum of the 12th Nguyen emperor, built on a hillside about 10 km south of Hue. Construction began in 1920 and took around 11 years, finishing in 1931 after the emperor’s death. Compared with the other tombs it is compact, but it has a striking presence.

    The tomb’s hallmark is its blend of Eastern and Western styles. Traditional Vietnamese decoration is combined with European-style concrete architecture and sculpture, giving it a look unlike the other tombs.

    Steps and exterior

    Steep flights of steps lead from the entrance to the main hall. Balustrades carved with dragons run beside each flight, and stone soldiers and mandarins line the courtyard. As you climb the view opens up over the valley below the hill.

    Over the years the outer walls have turned a mossy dark grey, which gives the tomb a heavy atmosphere. The contrast with the splendour inside is part of its charm.

    The dazzling interior

    Inside the main hall, walls and ceilings are covered with mosaics made from pieces of porcelain and glass, and gold decoration glitters. The finely painted scenes and patterns overwhelm the senses.

    At the centre sits a life-size bronze statue of Khai Dinh beneath a canopy, full of dignity. The emperor is said to be buried beneath the statue. Photography may be restricted inside, so follow the signs.

    Getting there and best time

    It is about 20 to 30 minutes by car or taxi from Hue city. The tomb is small and takes about an hour to visit, so it is easy to combine with other tombs.

    Mornings are quieter and the light is soft, which suits photography. Steps are slippery in the rain, so take care.

    Photo tips and nearby stops

    At Khai Dinh’s Tomb, popular shots include looking back from partway up the steps with the valley in frame, and close-ups of the interior mosaics. In the quiet morning you can photograph the whole staircase cleanly. Afterwards, stop at a simple local Hue eatery in a nearby village to enjoy the regional flavours.

    Because the tomb is compact, it is also relatively manageable for families with children and older travellers. The steps are steep, however, so use the handrails and take your time.

    Planning tips

    There are many steep steps, so wear comfortable shoes and climb at an easy pace. Cover shoulders and knees. Entry fees and opening hours may change.
